Capo Beach Plays By Its Own Rules

Capistrano Beach is its own little world inside Dana Point, a stack of bluff and sand between Doheny and the San Clemente line that locals just call Capo Beach. You will hear the market is slowing, and the headline numbers feed that story: roughly one listing in three is carrying a price cut, and there is enough inventory on the books to take a few months to clear at the current pace. But that is the high end and the overpriced listings dragging the average. The everyday Capo home, priced and shown well, is moving faster than it was a year ago, going pending in about nine days and closing right around asking. There is no single market right now. There is a fast lane and a slow lane, and which one your home lands in is mostly decided before it ever hits the MLS.

What's Happening in the Neighborhood

This Issue  |  Capistrano Beach

One ZIP, Three Different Markets

Capistrano Beach is the southern tip of Dana Point, between Doheny and the San Clemente line. Prices here swing widely, because one ZIP code covers three very different pockets. Closest to the highway are the smaller, older homes within walking distance of the sand, the most affordable way onto the coast and the quickest to sell, since almost nothing under 1.5 million on the water is left. On the bluff above sit the larger view homes, including the original 1920s Spanish-style houses, where an untouched original can trade right next to a multi-million-dollar remodel. And right on the sand is Beach Road, a private gated stretch where owners actually own the beach in front of their homes and the best oceanfront sells for ten million and up. Because so few homes change hands here in a given month, a single high-end sale, on Beach Road or in Dana Point enclaves like The Strand and Monarch Bay, can pull the median up by millions. That is why the monthly figures bounce, and why a jump or drop is usually about which homes happened to close, not the value of your own home moving. Over the long run, Capo keeps climbing.

Sand Is Coming Back to the Beach

The biggest local story is the shoreline. This month crews trucked in about 13,500 cubic yards of sand from a San Juan Capistrano quarry to slow the erosion that has been eating at Capistrano Beach Park, the rail line, and the parking lot since the 2018 boardwalk collapse. It is a stopgap ahead of the larger Capistrano-Doheny project, a 1,150-foot vegetated dune planned for the north end of the beach. If you own or are looking near the water, this is worth understanding before you ever talk price.

What's New in Capistrano Beach

Summer Lands on Doheny

The Doheny Surf and Art Festival returns to Doheny State Beach June 27 and 28, free, ten to five, with surf and art booths, live music, and food at the north edge of Capo Beach. The Ohana Festival comes back the weekend of September 25 with Pearl Jam and Eddie Vedder. Between events, Capo runs quieter than the Lantern District up the coast, and people like it that way: mornings at Beach Garden Cafe, coffee on the bluff at Pines Park, the day to day at Sprouts in Doheny Village. A good reminder of what people are really buying down here, the ability to walk to the water.

Waiting for rates to drop
before you buy?

My honest advice: stop waiting. Rates have sat in the mid-6s for a while, and the latest outlook does not have them falling anytime soon. The smarter play is to get creative on the financing instead.

One tool is a permanent rate buydown, often funded by a seller credit, which uses points at closing to lower the rate for the full life of the loan, not just the first year or two. As a rough rule of thumb, paying about one percent of the loan amount up front buys the rate down by roughly a quarter point, for as long as you own the home.
