"So how's the market
right now?"

When you get into real estate, you learn fast that this is the question everyone wants answered. Usually the response is something like "it's on fire" or "it's rough out there." Neither is really true, especially here. There are macro trends, of course, but what people are really asking is whether it's a good time to sell, and the honest answer depends on the house. Let me break it down.

The price more than doubles top to bottom while the size hardly moves. The two I marked are both four-bedrooms within 200 feet of each other, $2,085,000 apart. Now the days column: some sold the day they listed, one on Avenida Presidio took 49. Hot or slow? Same town, same year, both. There's no single number for what a home here is worth. There's the house, which is also why I don't trust the automated estimate on yours.

So, is it a good time to sell?

That's usually the real question, so here's the honest version. Over the past ten years, every town on this coast is up between 86 and 132 percent.

Newport Coast +132%

San Clemente +115%

Dana Point +113%

San Juan Capistrano +96%

Laguna Beach +86%

Orange County +93%

No one can promise you any single year, but in this pocket there's rarely a truly bad time to sell. Timing matters far less here than most places.

What matters more is the house, and two things move it. Price it to today's buyer, not last year's peak, since about a third of local listings have already had to cut. And show it ready to live in, because buyers don't want a project right now and the turn-key homes are the ones getting multiple offers. Those are the levers you control.

Worth Knowing  |  The Beach Trail

The Beach Trail is mostly walkable again, with one gap. The El Portal to Linda Lane stretch below Mariposa stays closed while OCTA finishes the catchment wall protecting the rail line, though the new pedestrian bridge over the Mariposa channel was just set in place. The city and OCTA expect a full reopening in late summer or early fall, no firm date yet. It's a big fix, about $310 million to keep the tracks and the trail where they are, and worth the patience.
