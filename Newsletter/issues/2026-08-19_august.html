A Note From Adam

Quick pause from real estate.

Saturday was my father-in-law's birthday.

We spent it at the Petrol Club in North Beach San Clemente, throwing him a party that doubled as a signing for his new book. It was the perfect venue to honor a racing legend.

His story is just remarkable. Racing is in his bloodline, and he made it to the top of American racing in the late '90s. He was fifth in points and coming off his first race win when a crash during a test in January of 2000 left him paralyzed from the neck down. Since then he has built one of the winningest teams in open-wheel racing history and driven a car he steers with his head at 213 miles an hour. And he isn't stopping yet.

In 2021 he walked my wife down the aisle with the help of an exoskeleton. Needless to say, pretty cool guy.

The book he wrote is called No Finish Line. It's a story of determination, unrelenting belief, and open-handed trust that God would use the injury for his glory. (And I'm not just saying that because he will read this email.)

What I keep coming back to is who showed up. Eighty or so people. Family, old friends, neighbors, people from all different chapters of our life, all standing shoulder to shoulder to celebrate Sam. What a gift it is to have such an amazing community, and to call San Clemente home.

Those last three are the whole story, and they look like they contradict each other. Homes priced at what the market is actually paying were gone in about ten days, slightly above ask. Meanwhile 44 percent of everything sitting out there right now has already had to come down, the highest reading I have on record for San Clemente. Same town, same month. The difference is almost entirely a decision made in the first week on the market.

One More Thing

I get asked every week whether to wait for rates to come down. A year ago the 30-year fixed averaged 6.70 percent. Last week it was 6.67. That is what a year of waiting bought. Three hundredths of a point.

The house did not wait, and that is the part that costs money. You can refinance a rate later. The price is the part you cannot renegotiate.
