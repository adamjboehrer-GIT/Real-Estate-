## The story

The split from last month sharpened rather than resolved. San Clemente is now clearing its entire active supply in 66 days, the fastest number in the peer set by a wide margin and 35 days better than a year ago. Demand is up 13% year over year while inventory is down 27%.

At the same time the share of active listings carrying a price cut went 34% to 40% to 44% across two decks. That is the highest San Clemente reading on record in this data set, and it is now running above the Orange County average of 41%.

Both are true. The homes that came out at a number the market recognizes closed in a median of 10 days at 100.4% of list, which is above ask. The homes that came out reaching are the ones sitting, cutting, and stretching the 66-day figure. The gap between those two outcomes is entirely a pricing decision made in week one.

That is the seller argument, and it is stronger now than it was a month ago because both halves of it got more extreme.

## Dana Point flipped

Worth noting because it changes the Dana Point page's whole framing. Dana Point's expected market time went 81 days (July 1) to 95 (July 28) to 140 (August 1), against 123 a year ago. It is now slower than it was last year, and it fell from second-fastest in the peer set to third.

It is not a supply problem. Inventory is down 19% year over year, 103 homes against 127. Pendings are down 29%. Demand fell faster than supply, which stretches the clock. Dana Point still carries the lowest price-cut share of the five at 28%, and its July closings came in at 99.6% of list.

## Transcription calls

Every deck has corrupted per-city cells. This one had seven, all resolved against the regional slides plus internal math (EMT = inventory / (demand / 30)). Full detail in the `notes` array of the JSON. The two that matter for copy:

- **San Clemente price cuts are 44%, not 45%.** The per-city price-reduction slides are shifted by one position from Laguna Beach onward, so San Clemente's own slide is showing San Juan Capistrano's number. The main Active Price Reductions slide and both regional slides agree on 44%.
- **Dana Point's 140-day EMT is correct.** Confirmed by the math: 103 homes / (22 pendings / 30) = 140.

Also corrected: Corona del Mar's row on the North Coastal slide (Aliso Viejo's data duplicated), Newport Beach's deltas on the same slide (Laguna Niguel's), Laguna Niguel's EMT on the South Coastal slide (Newport Beach's), Costa Mesa's 91.1% sale-to-list (should be 99.1%), Irvine's "55" $5M+ sales (should be 5), San Clemente's bucket split (64 / 1, not 65 / 2), and Corona del Mar's "+86%" Zillow Y-o-Y (should be +8.6%).

San Clemente single-family, July 2026: median $1,874,950 (+0.5% Y-o-Y), 9 median days active (from 30), 88 actives (-45.3%), 2.3 months supply (from 3.8), 39 new pendings (+14.7%), 99.0% of last list.

The single-family read reinforces the city-wide one. Supply is the constraint, not price. Actives are down 45% year over year to the tightest reading in the tracked series, absorption is three times faster than a year ago, and the median is flat. The market is being driven by how little there is to buy, not by appreciation.
