## The story

The headline is the split. San Clemente is clearing inventory in 70 days against 98 a year ago, which is the fastest on this stretch of coast by a wide margin. At the same time the share of listings that have taken a price cut jumped from 34% to 40% in four weeks. Those are not in tension. Demand is real and inventory is thin, but sellers who came out reaching are being made to come back to the number. Homes that priced correctly are still closing at about 99% of last list.

That is the whole seller argument this issue: the market is fast, but only for correctly-priced homes, and the penalty for testing a high number is now visible in the data.

## Why the issue leads with EMT, not DOM

Adam's call. Days on market only measures the homes that sold, so it systematically flatters a market by ignoring everything still sitting. Expected market time (active inventory divided by the current pace of pendings) covers the whole shelf. This deck's own numbers show the gap: San Clemente's median DOM on June closings was 10 days, while expected market time across all active supply is 70. Both are true; only one describes what a seller is actually walking into.

EMT is now the lead metric on the newsletter's "By The Numbers" block, the homepage, and both city market-report pages, replacing median days on market. DOM survives only where the copy explicitly contrasts the two.
