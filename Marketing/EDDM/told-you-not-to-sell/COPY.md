# EDDM Mailer — "The Realtor Who Told You Not to Sell"

**Status:** Copy approved by Adam 2026-07-16. Layout pending.
**Format:** 6.5" x 9" flat, two-sided, uncoated matte stock (see LAYOUT below).
**Concept:** The only piece in the mailbox that isn't asking for the listing. Adam
admits he has sent the same "now is the time" mail everyone else sends, then makes
the honest argument: the market doesn't decide when people sell, life does. The
payoff is a memory hook, not a call to action.

---

## SIDE A — Address side (seen first; carriers deliver address-up)

### Headline

> **Most of you shouldn't sell right now.**

Nothing else on this side except the required USPS address block and indicia.
No photo. No logo lockup competing with the line. The restraint is the credibility.

## SIDE B — Message side

### Letter (approved verbatim — do not rewrite without Adam)

> I know that's an odd thing to find in your mailbox. I've sent the other kind.
> Record prices, free valuation, now is the time. I've put my share of it in your
> mailbox myself.
>
> Here's what I've learned since. The market almost never decides when someone
> sells. Life does. A job moves. A parent needs help. A kid moves out and the house
> goes quiet, or one arrives and it gets small.
>
> If none of that is happening in your house, you probably shouldn't sell.
>
> When it does happen, it tends to arrive with everything else, and the house
> becomes one more thing to figure out at the worst possible time. That's the part
> I care about.
>
> So consider this an introduction. I'm the realtor who told you not to sell. And
> when the time does come, I'll be straight with you and outwork anyone to get your
> home the price it deserves.
>
> Adam

~140 words. Every cut sentence was one that repeated a beat the reader already had.

## LAYOUT

### Why the headline goes on the address side

USPS carriers deliver address-side up. The address side is what the recipient sees
first, standing over the recycling bin. Putting the headline on the "back" wastes it.

### Side A grid (6.5" x 9", portrait)

- Full-bleed white. (Brand: never black, never gold as a background.)
- Indicia: top right, 1/8" min from trim. EDDM Retail indicia box.
- Headline: centered horizontally, baseline at ~40% from top. Freight or Mercury
  serif, SIR Blue `#002349`, medium weight max. Large enough to read at arm's
  length but NOT filling the width — air around it is the design.
- Thin 1pt gold `#C29B40` rule, ~1.5" wide, centered, ~0.5" below the headline.
- Address block: bottom left, per USPS EDDM Retail spec.
  - "ECRWSS" endorsement
  - "Local Postal Customer"
  - Return address top left or with the block.
- Nothing else. No logo on this side.

### Side B grid

- Full-bleed white.
- Photo: top third. 3:2 ratio, min 1800x1200px. Adam in the neighborhood, NOT a
  studio headshot. No filters, no B&W, no text or logo overlaid on the photo.
- Letter: middle. Benton Sans (fallback Source Sans Pro), Text Grey `#666666`,
  generous leading. One thought per line where the line breaks allow. The piece must
  scan in ~8 seconds.
- "Adam" signoff: handwritten signature scan if available, else Freight italic.
- 1pt gold rule separating letter from bio block.
- Bio block: below the rule. Benton Sans, smaller.
- Pacific Sotheby's logo: bottom. (Brand: logo top or bottom only, never centered
  in the layout; clear space on all sides.)
- Contact line: bottom, Accent Grey `#999999` acceptable here (decorative/legal
  weight), or Text Grey. Never underline the URL.

### Stock

Uncoated matte. Everything else in an EDDM bundle is glossy 4-color card stock.
The format IS part of the differentiation — it should feel like a note, not an ad.
The real competitor is not another agent, it's the recycling bin between the mailbox
and the kitchen.

## USPS EDDM Retail constraints (verify current specs before print)

- Must qualify as a "flat": >6.125" high OR >11.5" long OR >0.25" thick.
  6.5" x 9" clears on height.
- Max 12" x 15", max 3.3 oz.
- Bundles of 50–100. Max 5,000 pieces per ZIP per day.
- Requires the ECRWSS endorsement and "Local Postal Customer" line.
- Select carrier routes at usps.com/eddm.
