# Market Moves — July 2026

**Reporting month:** July 2026, the most recent month in the data.
**Source:** California Regional MLS via InfoSparks (ShowingTime Plus). Residential, Single Family.
**Pulled:** August 17, 2026. Snapshot: `data/market_stats/2026-07_market_moves.json`.

---

## Headline

Supply is the whole story. Orange County is carrying 16 percent fewer active listings than a
year ago and San Clemente is down a startling 45 percent. Homes that are priced right are
moving fast, nine days in San Clemente against thirty a year ago, and closing at 99 percent
of the last list price. What supply has not done is push prices much. The San Clemente
median is essentially flat year over year. That combination, very little to buy and not much
price growth to show for it, is the tension worth explaining to anyone who asks.

**One caution before the numbers.** July is the newest month, and the flow metrics settle
late. Pending Sales are down sharply in all four markets this month, including 24.7 percent
year over year in Orange County. Some of that is real seasonality and some is late MLS status
posting. Treat New Listings, Pending Sales, and Closed Sales for July as soft. Active
Listings, Months Supply, and the price and pace figures are trustworthy.

---

## Orange County

- **Active listings 3,361**, down 15.9 percent from a year ago, up 2.0 percent from June.
- **Months supply 3.1**, against 3.7 a year ago. That is the low edge of balanced, tipping
  toward sellers.
- **Median sale price $1,450,000**, up 3.7 percent year over year, down 1.4 percent from June.
- **Median days on market 15**, against 17 a year ago.
- **Percent of last list price received 100 percent**, up from 99.1 a year ago.

The county is a tighter market than it was, but not a frantic one. Buyers are paying asking
price on average and the typical home is gone in about two weeks.

**The line for a client:** there is less to choose from than last year, and sellers who price
correctly are getting full asking, but the county is not running away on price.

## San Clemente

- **Active listings 88**, down 45.3 percent from 161 a year ago and down 13.7 percent from June.
  This is the standout number in the entire pull.
- **Months supply 2.3**, against 3.8 a year ago. Comfortably a seller's market.
- **Median days on market 9**, against 30 a year ago, a 70 percent drop.
- **Percent of last list price received 99 percent.**
- **Median sale price $1,874,950**, up 0.5 percent year over year.

That last figure needs framing rather than reporting. San Clemente closes fewer than fifty
single-family homes a month, so the monthly median swings hard on transaction mix. The last
four months read $2,050,000, $2,237,000, $1,892,500, $1,874,950. Any single month against
any other single month is noise. **The durable read is the trailing twelve-month average,
$1,882,991 against $1,839,708 for the prior twelve, up 2.4 percent.**

**The line for a client:** San Clemente has less than half the inventory it had a year ago
and well-priced homes are selling in nine days at 99 percent of ask, but prices are up only
about 2 percent over the year. Scarcity is showing up in speed, not in price.

## Dana Point

- **Active listings 74**, down 20.4 percent year over year but up 12.1 percent from June.
- **Months supply 3.7**, against 4.1 a year ago. Balanced.
- **Median days on market 38**, up from 30 a year ago and up sharply from June.
- **Median sale price $2,067,500**, down 12.9 percent year over year.
- **New listings 38**, up 18.8 percent year over year, the only market here adding supply.

Dana Point is the outlier and it is a mix story, not a price collapse. The trailing twelve-month
average is up on the prior year, and the monthly series has bounced between $2.07M and $4.35M
since January. July simply had a lower-priced mix. Pace is the more honest signal, and pace has
slowed: 38 days against 30 a year ago, in a market where inventory is now growing month to month.

**The line for a client:** Dana Point has loosened up. More new listings, more days to sell, and
the only market on this stretch where a buyer has gained a little leverage since June.

## Laguna Beach

- **Active listings 160**, down 20.4 percent year over year.
- **Months supply 7.4**, against 10.5 a year ago. Still a buyer's market on the standard reading,
  and by far the softest of the four, but meaningfully tighter than last year.
- **Median days on market 29**, against 55 a year ago, cut nearly in half.
- **Percent of last list price received 96.3 percent**, up from 94.8. Negotiation room here is
  real and it is the widest of the four markets.
- **Median sale price $3,350,000**, up 12.3 percent year over year.

**The line for a client:** Laguna is still the market where a buyer has the most room, roughly
four cents on the dollar off the last list price and seven months of supply, but it is tightening
fast and homes are selling in half the time they did a year ago.

## What changed that matters

1. **San Clemente inventory fell off a cliff.** 161 active listings a year ago, 88 now. Nothing
   else in this pull is close to that move.
2. **Speed improved everywhere except Dana Point.** San Clemente 30 days to 9, Laguna 55 to 29,
   Orange County 17 to 15. Dana Point went the other way, 30 to 38.
3. **Scarcity is not translating into price in San Clemente.** Down 45 percent on inventory,
   up 2.4 percent on the smoothed median. Worth saying out loud, because the instinct is to
   assume low supply means fast appreciation.
4. **Months supply tightened year over year in all four markets** while still being three
   genuinely different markets: San Clemente at 2.3, Orange County at 3.1, Dana Point at 3.7,
   Laguna Beach at 7.4.
5. **Pending sales dropped across the board in July.** Flagged above as partly a data artifact.
   Worth re-checking next month before drawing any conclusion from it.

---

## Data quality notes

- Single-month medians are unreliable at these transaction counts, especially San Clemente
  (48 closings) and Dana Point (24 closings). Every price claim above is framed against a
  multi-month average or explicitly labeled as a single month.
- Dana Point's trailing twelve-month average calculation is distorted by a run of very high
  months from October 2025 through May 2026, so the +32.7 percent trailing figure is not
  usable as a growth rate. Use pace and inventory for Dana Point commentary, not price.
- July flow metrics (New Listings, Pending Sales, Closed Sales) may revise upward as late
  status changes post.
