## Why the follow pass was held

Three things in that table argue against spending follow budget today.

**1. Eight posts converts badly.** A follow puts a notification in front of a
local business owner. They tap through and land on a profile with 8 posts and a
bio that does not name the market. Follow-back on that is roughly 5%. The same
follow against a profile with 30 or 40 posts and an obvious point of view runs
15–20%.

**2. The local pool is finite and each account is one shot.** Across the five
target cities there are maybe 400–800 accounts genuinely worth following. An
account that ignores a follow today cannot be re-followed later for a second
first impression. Spending that pool at 5% instead of 18% permanently wastes
most of it. This is the one growth mistake that cannot be undone.

**3. Following already exceeds followers (215 vs 187).** Adding 450 follows a
month that mostly do not convert pushes that ratio further the wrong way. For a
luxury brand, an account following far more than it has reads as a bot to both
the algorithm and to any homeowner who checks.

The plan doc already says the profile has to convert before traffic is worth
buying (`INSTAGRAM_PLAN.md`, "Step zero"). The baseline confirms it empirically.

## Recommended sequence

1. **Weeks 1–3: content only.** `/ig-content` weekly plus `/ig-reels` at 3–4 a
   week gets the feed to roughly 20 posts including 8–10 Reels. Reels also reach
   non-followers, so this is not a dormant period, it is the only phase where
   reach is free.
2. **Fix the bio now.** It does not name the geography. Someone arriving from a
   comment on a San Clemente business's post cannot tell Adam is local. Thirty
   seconds of work, and it gates the conversion rate of every loop that follows.
3. **Then turn on the follow and comment loops**, against a profile that converts
   three to four times better.
