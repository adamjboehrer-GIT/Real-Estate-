Coastal Currents · July 2026

A home that earns its keep.

San Clemente is clearing inventory faster than anywhere else on this stretch of coast, and four in ten listings have still had to cut their price. Both are true at once. Plus the honest answer to whether you can ever buy here, and the Beach Trail reopening timeline.

Published July 29, 2026 by Adam Boehrer, Real Estate Agent, Pacific Sotheby’s International Realty, DRE #02419464.

A Note From Adam

Living in South Orange County almost feels unfair.

Almost.

Beach days are endless. The weather is perfect. New restaurants and venues pop up every month.

It's sunshine and rainbows until you're doomscrolling Zillow at 11pm, looking at two-million-dollar shacks built in the sixties that you still can't afford. Then the question shows up on its own. Will we ever be able to buy a house here?

I'll answer that for you.

Yes.

It won't be the cute white-picket-fence single-family home you've been imagining. It'll be a home that earns its keep. Call it house hacking, call it owner-occupied living, call it whatever you want. I call it the best way to get you into owning a home here.

Two things make it more doable than it looks. Because you'd live there, it counts as your primary home, which opens up loans that get you in for far less than the 20 percent everyone assumes. And be honest about what you already pay. A lot of people down here hand a landlord a mortgage-sized check every month. This just points that same money at something you own.

The Good Life

The Beach Trail is coming back.

Good news for anyone missing their walk: the Beach Trail is coming back. OCTA has set the new pedestrian bridge over the Mariposa channel, and the roughly 1,400-foot wall being built to protect the rail line from the hillside above keeps moving along. The stretch from El Portal to Linda Lane stays closed until the work wraps, while the northern segment reopens most evenings and all day Sunday. When it's finished, the trail gets rebuilt on a raised alignment right beside the tracks, coastal views intact. The city and OCTA are pointing to late summer for a full reopening, no firm date yet, but the finish line is finally in sight.

By The Numbers

San Clemente, July 2026

Two things are true at once right now. The town is selling through its inventory faster than it was a year ago, and four in ten listings have still had to cut their price.

  • 70

    Days of expected market time, down from 98 a year ago

  • 40%

    Of active listings have taken a price cut, up from 34% four weeks ago

  • $1.89M

    Median June sale price, up 3.4% year over year

Expected market time is the one I watch. Days on market only counts the homes that actually sold, so it quietly ignores everything still sitting. Expected market time takes every home currently for sale and the pace buyers are absorbing them, and answers how long it would take to clear the whole shelf. San Clemente is at 70 days. A year ago it was 98.

The price cuts are not a contradiction. Homes that came out at a number the market recognized are closing at about 99 percent of their last list price. The ones that came out reaching are the ones cutting. With active inventory down almost 40 percent from a year ago, there are not many homes in play, and the well-priced ones are still getting picked off quickly.
