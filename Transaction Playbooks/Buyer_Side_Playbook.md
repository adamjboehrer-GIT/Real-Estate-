# Buyer-Side Transaction Playbook

<div class="subtitle">From accepted offer to recorded deed. Coastal Orange County and North San Diego County.</div>

<div class="callout">
<p class="ct">The one idea this document is built on</p>
<p>The moment an offer is accepted, your job changes. Up to that point you were a salesperson. From that point forward you are a <strong>risk manager, a calendar, and a translator</strong>. Almost every transaction that falls apart does so for one of three reasons, and all three are inside your control:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A date got missed</strong> because nobody was tracking it.</li>
<li><strong>A condition surfaced late</strong> because nobody went looking early.</li>
<li><strong>The client found out from someone else</strong> because you went quiet.</li>
</ul>
<p>Every checklist, question, and script below exists to prevent one of those three.</p>
</div>

## How to use this

Work top to bottom on a live deal. The phase sections are chronological. The question banks in the back half are meant to be read out loud, verbatim, on the phone. The deal-killer table is worth re-reading at day 10 of every escrow, when you still have time to act on what it says.

Two standing rules that apply to every page:

**Rule 1: Calendar from the executed contract, never from this document.** The day counts here are the printed defaults on the C.A.R. Residential Purchase Agreement. Every one of them is a fill-in blank that gets negotiated per deal, and C.A.R. has materially revised the RPA three times in the last eighteen months. Open the actual signed contract, read the actual numbers, and build the calendar from those.

**Rule 2: Nothing in California happens automatically.** Contingencies do not expire on their own. A passed deadline does not remove a contingency, does not cancel a contract, and does not release a deposit. Every one of those requires a signed written notice delivered to the other side. This protects your buyer past day 17, and it is also why the seller can come after your buyer's deposit if you are sloppy.

## The master timeline

Acceptance is Day 0, and Day 0 is the day the last signature is **delivered back** to the offering party, not the day it was signed. Getting this date wrong shifts every date downstream and is the single most common rookie error in the business.

| Day | What is due | Who owes it | Form |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Acceptance delivered. Clock starts. | — | RPA |
| 1 | Contract to escrow and title. Escrow opened. | Both agents | — |
| 1 | Wire fraud conversation, by phone | You | WFA |
| 3 | Earnest money deposit into escrow | Buyer | RPA ¶3 |
| 5–7 | Seller delivers disclosure package | Seller | TDS, SPQ, AVID, NHD, FLD |
| 7 | Seller delivers HOA documents | Seller | HOA-required |
| 3–10 | All physical inspections completed | Buyer | — |
| 10–14 | Appraisal ordered and inspected | Lender | — |
| 12–15 | Request for Repair delivered | Buyer | RR |
| 17 | Investigation contingency removal | Buyer | CR |
| 17 | Loan and appraisal contingency removal | Buyer | CR |
| 21–25 | Loan approval final, conditions cleared | Lender | — |
| 25–28 | Closing Disclosure issued, docs to escrow | Lender | CD |
| 27–29 | Final verification of property condition | Buyer | VP |
| 28–29 | Buyer signs, funds wired | Buyer | — |
| 30 | Funding and recording. Keys. | — | — |

<div class="callout">
<p class="ct">Negotiate the clock before acceptance, not after</p>
<p>These defaults are frequently wrong for your market. A 17-day investigation window is not enough for a 1968 hillside house in San Clemente that needs a general, a sewer scope, a roof, and a geotechnical review. A 17-day loan contingency is optimistic on a $2.8M jumbo in a slow underwriting month. You fix this when you write the offer. Once it is accepted, you are asking for a favor instead of setting a term.</p>
</div>

## Phase 1 — Days 0 to 3: The opening move

Most agents waste this window. Everything you do here compounds, because it either creates or destroys the other side's confidence in you at exactly the moment they are deciding how much rope to give you later.

### Your action list

1. **Confirm the acceptance date in writing** with the listing agent. One line: *"Confirming full execution delivered [date], so contingencies run to [date]. Agree?"* Get it in an email. You now have a paper record if there is ever a dispute.
2. **Deliver the executed contract to escrow and title within 24 hours.** Then call and confirm a human has it. Do not assume the email landed.
3. **Get the escrow number, the escrow officer's direct line, and the escrow assistant's name.** The assistant is who actually answers.
4. **Make the wire fraud call.** Not an email, not the advisory in the packet. A call, in your voice. Script is in the question bank below.
5. **Calendar every date** from the contract into your calendar and your client's, with alerts at 3 days out and 1 day out.
6. **Send the client a plain-English roadmap.** One page. Not the contract. What happens, when, what you need from them and by when.
7. **Get the EMD wired and get proof.** A late deposit is the cleanest possible reason for a seller to serve a Notice to Buyer to Perform and start unwinding your deal. There is no excuse for this one.
8. **Call the lender the same day.** Confirm the file is submitted to underwriting, not "being worked on."
9. **Book inspections immediately.** Good general inspectors, sewer scope techs, roofers, and structural engineers on this coast book out one to two weeks. If you are calling on day 8 of a 17-day period, you have already lost.
10. **Order the preliminary title report** and actually read it when it arrives.

<div class="script">
<p class="ct">The wire fraud call. Say this, twice per escrow.</p>
<p>"I need ninety seconds on the most important thing I will tell you during this whole process. Wire fraud in real estate is real, it is common in our price range, and it is unrecoverable. Here is the rule: <strong>nobody will ever email you changed wiring instructions.</strong> Not escrow, not me, not the title company. If you receive an email with wire instructions or a change to wire instructions, do not act on it. Call me, and separately call escrow at the number I am giving you right now, which you should save in your phone today. Not a number from an email. Do we have that understood?"</p>
<p>Make this call at open of escrow, and again the week funds are due.</p>
</div>

## Phase 2 — Days 3 to 17: The investigation window

This is where the deal is genuinely won or lost, and where you produce the most value you will produce all year.

### Attend every inspection

Not "review the report." Be physically there. Inspectors say things out loud that get softened into hedged language in the written report, and you will understand severity in a way a PDF will never give you. It is also where you build enormous trust, because you are the one standing in the crawlspace with your buyer instead of forwarding a file.

### Order the right inspections

The general home inspection is the floor, not the ceiling. On this coast, in this price range, the following are situational but frequently necessary:

| Inspection | Order it when |
|---|---|
| General home inspection | Always. No exceptions. |
| Sewer lateral camera scope | Anything pre-1990. Clay and cast iron laterals under mature ficus and eucalyptus are the coastal norm. |
| Termite / WDO | Always. Coastal moisture plus older wood framing. |
| Roof | Any flat or low-slope roof, any deck over living space, any roof over 15 years old. Deck-over-living-space leaks are the classic coastal defect. |
| Chimney | Any fireplace, and any pre-1994 masonry (Northridge-era cracking). |
| Structural / geotechnical engineer | Any slope, bluff proximity, retaining wall, or stair-step stucco cracking. San Clemente and Dana Point sit on documented ancient landslide terrain. This is a real risk, not a theoretical one. |
| Pool and spa | Any pool. Equipment and plaster life are both expensive. |
| Septic and leach field | Capo Beach, unincorporated county pockets, older Laguna canyon. |
| Well | Rural and canyon parcels. |
| HVAC specialist | Systems over 12 years, or any zoned or mini-split system. |
| Mold / moisture | Any staining, any prior leak disclosed, any musty smell. |

### Read the paper like a lawyer

Cross-reference the disclosures against the inspection findings. This is the part almost nobody does well.

- **TDS and SPQ** against the inspection report. If the SPQ says "no known leaks" and the inspector photographs water staining at a ceiling, you have both a conversation to have and a leverage point.
- **Preliminary title report.** You are hunting for: easements, CC&R restrictions, mechanics' liens, solar UCC-1 filings, PACE/HERO assessments, and Mello-Roos or 1915 Act bonds.
- **NHD report.** Flood, fire severity, earthquake fault, and liquefaction zones. Fire zone status is now an insurance problem, not a paperwork problem.
- **HOA documents.** Budget, reserve study, minutes for the last twelve months, and any pending or contemplated special assessment. The minutes are where the truth lives.

### Translate, do not opine

You are not a contractor and you must not act like one. What you do is sort the report into three buckets for your buyer:

1. **Material defects** that change the value or the safety of the house.
2. **Deferred maintenance** the buyer is knowingly buying.
3. **Inspector CYA noise** that appears in every report ever written.

Then get real bids on bucket one. A Request for Repair backed by a licensed contractor's written bid gets paid. A request based on "the inspector said maybe ten to fifteen thousand" gets laughed at and costs you credibility for the rest of the deal.

### Ask for credit, not repair

Default to a credit in almost every case. A credit is certain and it is yours. A seller-performed repair is done by the cheapest bidder under time pressure, and then you own the warranty argument forever. The exceptions: anything the lender requires be corrected before funding, and genuine health and safety items.

## Phase 3 — Day 17: Contingency removal

Removal is signed on form CR and it is functionally irreversible. Never do it by text. Sit with your client in person or get them on the phone.

<div class="script">
<p class="ct">The contingency removal conversation</p>
<p>"Before you sign this I want to make sure you understand exactly what changes. Right now, if you walked away, you would get your deposit back. The moment you sign this, that stops being true. If you walk after this, we are arguing over your deposit, which is <strong>$[actual dollar figure]</strong>. Say that number back to me so I know we are on the same page. Is there anything at all still unresolved in your mind about the house, the loan, or the money?"</p>
</div>

**Do not remove until all four are true:**

1. Inspections are satisfactory or resolved.
2. Disclosures are reviewed and satisfactory.
3. The appraisal is in at or above value, or the gap is resolved.
4. Loan approval is in hand with no conditions that scare you.

If any one is missing, **request an extension in writing before the deadline, not after.** An extension requested on day 16 is housekeeping. One requested on day 18 is a negotiation you are losing.

You can also remove contingencies individually. If inspections are clean but the loan is still moving, remove investigation and hold the loan contingency. That is a real good-faith signal to the seller that costs your buyer nothing.

## Phase 4 — Days 17 to 27: Getting it funded

This is the quiet stretch where deals die from inattention. Your job is a status pull every two to three days, minimum.

### The client behavior briefing

Say this at contingency removal, and again a week before close. Lenders re-pull credit and re-verify employment days before funding, and this kills more deals in practice than inspections do:

- **Do not open any credit account.** Not a furniture card, not a store card at the register.
- **Do not buy a car.** This is not a joke and it happens constantly.
- **Do not change jobs**, take a new title, or switch from W-2 to 1099.
- **Do not move large sums between accounts.**
- **Do not deposit unsourced cash.** Every dollar needs a paper trail.
- **Do not pay off a collection or close a card** without asking the lender first. It can move the score the wrong direction.

### Insurance is now a real transaction risk

Start this in week one, not week four. In wildfire-exposed areas (Laguna canyon, San Clemente's eastern edges, anything abutting open space), admitted carriers are declining and non-renewing, which pushes buyers to the FAIR Plan plus a DIC wrapper. That is expensive, slow, and can materially change the payment and therefore the loan approval.

The current RPA clarifies that the **loan contingency does not cover insurance problems.** If insurability is a live question on a property, you want a written insurance contingency or, at minimum, a bound quote before you remove anything.

### Final verification of property condition

Form VP. Schedule as close to closing as reasonable, and walk it properly:

- Run every faucet and flush every toilet
- Test the AC and the heat, both
- Open and close the garage door with the remote
- Confirm all agreed repairs are done, with receipts in hand
- Confirm nothing was removed that was supposed to convey (fixtures, mounts, shelving, appliances)
- Photograph anything that is wrong, immediately, on site

### Review the Closing Disclosure line by line

Against the contract. Purchase price, every credit, proration, compensation, and who pays what per the terms. Errors here are common and they are yours to catch.

---

## Phase 5 — Close and after

- Confirm **funding** and then confirm **recording**. They are two different events and only the second one transfers the property.
- Keys, remotes, gate fobs, mailbox keys, and any HOA amenity cards or codes.
- Utilities transferred effective the recording date.
- Hand off warranties, manuals, and the vendor list.
- Set a reminder for the **supplemental tax bill**, which arrives months later and shocks people who were not warned.
- Remind them to file the **homeowner's exemption**.
- Check in at 30 days, 6 months, and every anniversary.

<p class="ct">The transaction is where you earned the commission. The twelve months after is where you earn the next three.</p>

# Question banks

Read these out loud. Specific questions get specific answers; "how's it going" gets "fine," which is not information.

## Ask the lender — every 2 to 3 days

<div class="qa">
<ul>
<li>Is the file <strong>in underwriting</strong>, or is it still in processing? Which one, today?</li>
<li>Has it been through underwriting <strong>once or twice</strong>? What came back?</li>
<li>What conditions are outstanding right now, and who owes each one, the borrower or you?</li>
<li>Is the appraisal <strong>ordered</strong>, and has it been <strong>inspected</strong>? What date did the appraiser physically go out?</li>
<li>Has the appraisal been <strong>reviewed</strong> by the lender, or just received?</li>
<li>When will the Closing Disclosure be issued? (This starts a mandatory 3-business-day clock before signing.)</li>
<li>Are we clear to close, or clear to close <em>with conditions</em>? Name the conditions.</li>
<li>When do docs go to escrow, and when is the wire cut?</li>
<li>Is there anything about this file that would keep you up at night if it were your deal?</li>
</ul>
</div>

That last question is the most valuable one in this document. It gets a lender to volunteer the thing they were hoping not to have to mention.

## Ask the escrow officer

<div class="qa">
<ul>
<li>Is the deposit in, and can you send me written confirmation?</li>
<li>Do you have all signed documents from both sides? What is missing?</li>
<li>Do you have the compensation instructions from both brokerages?</li>
<li>Is title clear? Are there any items on the prelim that still need to be cleared, and who is clearing them?</li>
<li>Has the payoff demand been ordered and received? Is there more than one lien?</li>
<li>Have HOA transfer documents and the certification been ordered? When were they ordered?</li>
<li>Are there any liens, judgments, or child support liens that came up in the search?</li>
<li>What is still outstanding on your side to be able to close on time?</li>
<li>What day do you need my buyer to sign, and where?</li>
</ul>
</div>

## Ask the title officer

<div class="qa">
<ul>
<li>Walk me through the exceptions on Schedule B. Which of these actually affect use of the property?</li>
<li>Are there any easements that cross the buildable area?</li>
<li>Is there a solar UCC-1 filing, or a PACE or HERO assessment on the tax roll?</li>
<li>Is there a Mello-Roos or 1915 Act bond, and what is the current annual amount and remaining term?</li>
<li>Are there any private transfer fees recorded against the property?</li>
<li>Any recorded notices, violations, or open permits you can see?</li>
</ul>
</div>

## Ask the listing agent

<div class="qa">
<ul>
<li>Confirming acceptance date and the resulting contingency dates. Do you agree with these?</li>
<li>When will the disclosure package be delivered? Can I get the prelim and HOA docs early?</li>
<li>Is the seller occupying? Do they need a rent-back, and how long?</li>
<li>What does the seller's move-out timeline actually look like?</li>
<li>Are there any known permit issues, unpermitted work, or open permits?</li>
<li>What is the seller's biggest concern about this deal closing?</li>
<li>Have there been prior inspections from a fallen-out escrow that you can share?</li>
</ul>
</div>

That last one is free money. A previous buyer's inspection report tells you where the bodies are before you spend a dollar.

## Ask the inspector, on site

<div class="qa">
<ul>
<li>If this were your house, what would you fix first, and what would you not bother with?</li>
<li>Which of these items is a symptom of something bigger that I should get a specialist on?</li>
<li>What is the realistic remaining life on the roof, the HVAC, the water heater, and the electrical panel?</li>
<li>Is there any evidence of a repair that was done to cover something rather than fix it?</li>
<li>Is there anything you saw that you are going to write up softly, but that I should understand is serious?</li>
</ul>
</div>

## Ask the insurance agent, in week one

<div class="qa">
<ul>
<li>Can you write this address with an admitted carrier, or are we looking at FAIR Plan plus a DIC policy?</li>
<li>What is the fire severity zone designation, and how does it affect the premium?</li>
<li>Has this address had prior claims that show in the CLUE report?</li>
<li>What is the actual annual premium, so I can give the lender a real number instead of an estimate?</li>
<li>How long will binding take, and what do you need from us to bind?</li>
</ul>
</div>

## Ask HOA management

<div class="qa">
<ul>
<li>Are there any special assessments currently in effect, approved, or under discussion?</li>
<li>Is there any pending or threatened litigation involving the association?</li>
<li>What is the reserve funding percentage as of the most recent study?</li>
<li>Has the association completed its SB 326 balcony and elevated element inspection, and what were the findings?</li>
<li>Are there any open violations against this unit?</li>
<li>What are the transfer fees, and how long does document delivery take?</li>
<li>Are there rental restrictions, minimum lease terms, or a rental cap with a waiting list?</li>
</ul>
</div>

## Ask your buyer, at acceptance

<div class="qa">
<ul>
<li>What would have to be true for you to want out of this house?</li>
<li>What is your actual ceiling if repairs or the appraisal move the number?</li>
<li>Are you expecting any change in employment, income structure, or credit in the next 45 days?</li>
<li>Is any part of your down payment coming from a gift, the sale of an asset, or a retirement account? (All three need lead time.)</li>
<li>Who else is going to have an opinion about this purchase, and when do they want to see the house?</li>
<li>How do you want to receive updates, and how often?</li>
</ul>
</div>

The "who else will have an opinion" question defuses the parent, the sibling, or the contractor friend who shows up on day 15 and blows up the deal.

# What kills deals, and what you control

| Deal killer | Early warning sign | What you control | The mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loan falls apart | Lender is vague, file is "in processing" past day 7, conditions keep multiplying | Everything about the pace of information | Weekly minimum lender calls with the specific script. Require fully underwritten pre-approval at offer time, not a pre-qual. |
| Buyer credit event | Client mentions new furniture, a car, a job offer | The briefing | Deliver the behavior briefing twice, in writing and verbally. Frame it as "the lender will re-pull days before funding." |
| Low appraisal | Thin comps, aggressive price, unique property | Preparation | Know the comps before you write the offer. On the buyer side, negotiate an appraisal gap term rather than an all-or-nothing contingency so the outcome is pre-agreed. |
| Insurance unavailable or unaffordable | Fire severity zone on the NHD, canyon or open-space adjacency | Timing | Quote insurance in week one. The loan contingency does not cover insurance under the current RPA. |
| Inspection shock | Older home, deferred maintenance, coastal exposure | Expectation setting | Prepare the buyer before the inspection that every house on this coast generates a list. Sort into three buckets. Get bids. |
| Missed deadline | Nobody built a calendar | Entirely yours | Calendar everything on day 0 with 3-day and 1-day alerts. Request extensions before the date, never after. |
| Late-surfacing HOA assessment | Coastal condo, older building, balconies | Diligence | Read the minutes and the reserve study, not just the budget. Ask management the special assessment question directly. |
| Title defect | Unusual vesting, trust, probate, divorce, prior lien | Early reading | Read the prelim the day it arrives. Escalate anything unclear to the title officer immediately. |
| Solar surprise | Panels on the roof | Asking on day one | Determine on day 1 which of the four it is: owned, financed, leased, or PPA. Leases require assumption approval and take weeks. |
| Seller cannot move out | Vague answers about the seller's next home | Asking directly | Ask the listing agent about the move-out plan at acceptance. Paper any rent-back properly. |
| Wire fraud | None. It just happens. | The conversation | The phone call, twice. |
| Client emotional exit | Silence, slow responses, second-guessing | Cadence | Fixed weekly update whether or not there is news. Narrate the next step before it happens. |

<div class="callout">
<p class="ct">Own the other side of the deal too</p>
<p>If the listing agent is disorganized, you do not get to shrug and say they dropped the ball. Track their dates. Remind them. Offer to send the form. Your duty is to your client's outcome, and your client does not care whose fault it was. This one habit also makes you the agent everyone wants across the table, which is worth more than any lead source you will ever buy.</p>

# Details that get lost

The list of things that are nobody's obvious job, which means they become your job.

**Contract and money**

- The true acceptance date, confirmed in writing with the other agent
- Written proof the EMD actually landed in escrow, not the other agent's word
- Whether the deposit is being increased at contingency removal, if that was negotiated
- Exactly what the appraisal gap language obligates your buyer to do
- Whether repairs were agreed as a credit or as seller-performed work, and in writing either way
- Proration of taxes, HOA dues, and any rent if tenant-occupied
- Compensation instructions delivered to escrow from your brokerage

**Property and condition**

- Personal property that was included: appliances, TV mounts, shelving, patio furniture, window coverings
- What conveys versus what is a fixture, written down before it becomes an argument
- Whether agreed repairs actually got done, with receipts, before the walkthrough
- All keys, remotes, gate fobs, mailbox keys, pool keys, HOA cards and codes
- Alarm codes, smart-home account transfers, and garage keypad codes

**Ownership and legal**

- **Title vesting.** How your buyer takes title has tax and estate consequences and must be decided before docs are drawn, not at the signing table. Send them to their attorney or CPA. Do not advise on this yourself.
- Trust or LLC documentation, if the buyer is taking title in an entity. This takes lead time.
- **FinCEN reporting** on all-cash purchases by entities and trusts. The reporting language is now built into the RPA itself rather than a standalone form.
- Water heater strapping and smoke and CO detector compliance, plus any city-specific point-of-sale requirement
- Any city retrofit or point-of-sale ordinance. Confirm per city rather than assuming.

**Money after close**

- **Supplemental tax bill.** It arrives months later, it is not in the impound account, and unwarned buyers are furious about it.
- **Homeowner's exemption** filing
- Mello-Roos or 1915 Act bond annual amounts, understood as part of the real payment
- HOA payment setup and the first due date
- Utility transfer effective the recording date

# Southern California specifics

**Mello-Roos and 1915 Act bonds.** Talega, Rancho Mission Viejo, Ladera Ranch, parts of Ocean Ridge. A separate statutory disclosure, and it can add several hundred dollars a month, which affects debt-to-income and can blow up a loan approval late in escrow. Pull the actual tax bill early on any post-1990 development. Know the total assessment, not the base rate.

**SB 326 balcony and elevated element inspections.** Every condo HOA in California has had to inspect elevated wood elements. Coastal salt air means findings are common and repairs are expensive. On any coastal condo, read the minutes and the reserve study for balcony repair special assessments. A $40,000 special assessment discovered after contingency removal is a nightmare you created.

**Coastal Zone permitting.** Much of San Clemente, Dana Point, Laguna, Carlsbad, and Oceanside sits in the Coastal Zone. Unpermitted additions, decks, and seawall work are live issues, and a Coastal Development Permit is not a formality. "We added the deck ourselves" means a permit search at the city and a real disclosure conversation.

**Bluff and slope stability.** San Clemente in particular has a documented landslide and bluff-retreat history, and the rail corridor stabilization work has kept it in the news. On any bluff-adjacent or hillside property, a geotechnical review during the investigation period is money well spent. Insurability and future permitting both hinge on it.

**Solar, four flavors.** Owned, financed, leased, and PPA are four completely different transactions. Lease assumption requires solar company approval and takes weeks. PACE is a tax-lien-position assessment that most lenders require be paid off at close. Find out which one on day 1.

**Supplemental taxes and Prop 19.** Warn every buyer about the supplemental bill. And on the seller side of a trade-up client, Prop 19 base-year transfer for 55+ owners is genuinely valuable and most agents never raise it. Send them to their CPA, but be the one who brought it up.

# C.A.R. form cheat sheet

| Form | What it is | When you use it |
|---|---|---|
| RPA | Residential Purchase Agreement | The contract |
| BRBC | Buyer Representation and Broker Compensation | Before showing |
| TDS | Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement | Seller delivers |
| SPQ | Seller Property Questionnaire | Seller delivers |
| AVID | Agent Visual Inspection Disclosure | Both agents complete |
| SBSA | Statewide Buyer and Seller Advisory | With the offer |
| BIA | Buyer's Inspection Advisory | With the offer |
| WFA | Wire Fraud and Electronic Funds Advisory | Day 1 |
| FLD | Lead-Based Paint Disclosure | Any pre-1978 property |
| NHD | Natural Hazard Disclosure | Seller-ordered report |
| WHSD | Water Heater and Smoke Detector Statement | Before close |
| RR | Request for Repair | After inspections |
| CR | Contingency Removal | Day 17-ish |
| NBP | Notice to Buyer to Perform | Seller's tool |

| CC | Cancellation of Contract | To unwind |
| SIP | Seller in Possession | Rent-back 30 days or less |

*Form availability and content change. Verify against your current C.A.R. library before relying on any of the above.*
